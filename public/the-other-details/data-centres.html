The cloud has an address.

Every reel you scroll lands in a building. India now has about 1,500 MW of these buildings, most in a few water-short cities. Here is what they burn and drink.

Before you start

Three words you'll need.

Data centre
A building full of servers that store and process what we do online.
MW and GW
Megawatts and gigawatts measure electrical power. 1 GW is 1,000 MW. Data centre size is quoted by the power it is built to draw.
Water stress
When people and industry draw a high share of the water that nature renews each year in a region.
01 · Growth

Four times, in five years.

Installed capacity rose from about 375 MW in 2020 to about 1,500 MW by 2025. Drawn to scale, the 2025 square is exactly four 2020 squares.

Why it matters. Private AI investment in India grew from $1.2 bn to $4.1 bn in a single year, and AI needs far more computing than ordinary apps.

Sources · MeitY, Digital India · World Bank, India Development Update 2026

02 · Power

Small for now. Nobody agrees what's next.

Data centres use about half a percent of India's electricity. A CEEW study expects 4.5 to 6.5 GW by 2030; the power ministry now projects an additional 26.3 GW of AI data centre load by FY 2032. On the chart, the real trend is solid and the forecasts are dashed.

How to read it. The two forecasts measure slightly different things, installed capacity and power demand, and still differ by four times or more. That gap is the uncertainty.

Sources · CEEW & SYSTEMIQ 2026 · Ministry of Power, Parliament reply, Jul 2026

03 · Water

One campus, one town.

Chips turn electricity into heat, and the cheapest way to remove heat is evaporating water. A 100 MW campus can use about 20 lakh litres a day. Divided by a city's planning norm of 135 litres per person, that is about 15,000 people.

Across India. About 150 billion litres in 2024. That is the daily water of about 3 million people, every day of the year. It is expected to more than double by 2030.

Sources · CEEW & SYSTEMIQ 2026 · CPHEEO norm

04 · Stress

Built where water is short.

More than half of India's data centres already sit in water-stressed regions. India has about 18% of the world's people and about 4% of its fresh water.

What could change. Closed-loop cooling, cooling at the chip and treated city wastewater all cut fresh water. Only 5 of 15 state data centre policies ask for sustainability today.

Sources · WRI India 2026 · S&P Global 2025 · Jal Shakti Ministry · CEEW & SYSTEMIQ 2026

Key takeaways
  1. The internet is physical: India's data centre capacity grew four times between 2020 and 2025.
  2. Power use is small today, and forecasts for the next decade differ by four times or more.
  3. Water is the hidden cost: one large campus needs as much as a town of 15,000 people, mostly in water-short regions.
