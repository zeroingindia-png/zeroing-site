The gold at home.

Indians bought less gold by weight in 2025 and paid more for it than ever. Families rarely sell. More of them now borrow against it.

Before you start

Three words you'll need.

Tonne
1,000 kilograms. Gold demand is counted in tonnes; prices in rupees per 10 grams.
Above-ground stock
All the gold ever mined that still exists, in jewellery, bars, coins and vaults.
Gold loan
Borrowing money by pledging jewellery as security. The gold stays with the lender until the loan is repaid.
01 · The paradox

Less gold, more money.

Demand fell to 710.9 tonnes, down 11%. Its value rose 30% to ₹7.51 lakh crore. Jewellery fell 24% by weight and still set a spending record of $49 bn.

Why. Local gold prices rose 74% in 2025. Families paid more for less metal.

Sources · World Gold Council, Gold Demand Trends FY2025

02 · The stock

Homes hold more than any central bank.

All the gold ever mined is about 222,600 tonnes. Estimates for Indian homes run from 25,000 to 34,600 tonnes, roughly one gram in every eight. The RBI holds 880.5.

Why we don't print one number. The "25,000 tonnes" quoted everywhere is a 2019 estimate built on a study from about 2017. The newer "34,600 tonnes" does not reconcile with its own valuation, which implies about 29,000.

Sources · World Gold Council · RBI · estimates in the ledger below

03 · Holding on

Families borrow against it. They rarely sell.

Bank loans against gold jewellery were reported at about ₹5.1 lakh crore in May 2026, roughly double a year earlier. Gold ETFs, funds that track the gold price, took ₹24,039 crore in a single month.

Checked, with care. The gold-loan figure comes from press reports of RBI data, so the ledger marks it "Check" until we confirm it in the RBI release. The government stopped new medium and long term deposits under the Gold Monetisation Scheme in March 2025, after it gathered about 31 tonnes.

Sources · RBI sectoral data, as reported · AMFI · Finance Ministry

Key takeaways
  1. India bought 11% less gold by weight in 2025 but spent 30% more on it.
  2. Indian homes hold far more gold than the RBI, though the exact amount is an estimate with a wide range.
  3. Families are borrowing against gold and buying it as funds, rather than selling it.
