The gold at home.

Indians bought less gold by weight in 2025 and paid more for it than ever. Families rarely sell. More of them now borrow against it.

Before you start

Three words you'll need.

Tonne
1,000 kilograms. Gold demand is counted in tonnes; prices in rupees per 10 grams.
Above-ground stock
All the gold ever mined that still exists, in jewellery, bars, coins and vaults.
Gold loan
Borrowing money by pledging jewellery as security. The gold stays with the lender until the loan is repaid.
01 · The paradox

Less gold, more money.

Demand fell to 710.9 tonnes, down 11%. Its value rose 30% to ₹7.51 lakh crore. Jewellery fell 24% by weight and still set a spending record of $49 bn.

Why. The average gold price in rupees was 67% higher in 2025 than in 2024. Families paid more for less metal.

Sources · World Gold Council, Gold Demand Trends FY2025 · average price as reported by PTI

02 · The stock

Homes hold more than any central bank.

All the gold ever mined is about 222,600 tonnes. Estimates for Indian homes run from 25,000 to 34,600 tonnes, roughly one gram in every eight. The RBI holds 880.5.

Why we don't print one number. The "25,000 tonnes" quoted everywhere is a 2019 estimate built on a study from about 2017. The newer "34,600 tonnes" does not reconcile with its own valuation, which implies about 29,000.

Sources · World Gold Council · RBI · estimates in the ledger below

03 · Holding on

Families borrow against it. They rarely sell.

Bank loans against gold jewellery reached ₹5.77 lakh crore by September 2026, 83% more than a year earlier. Gold ETFs, funds that track the gold price, now hold about ₹1.86 lakh crore.

Checked, with care. RBI notes that one bank moved some farm loans into this category, which inflates the growth rate. The government's Gold Monetisation Scheme gathered about 38 tonnes before its main parts closed in March 2025.

Sources · RBI, Sectoral Deployment of Bank Credit, Sep 2026 · AMFI, Sep 2026 · Finance Ministry, Rajya Sabha reply 2025

04 · What the headlines miss

Bought, pawned, rarely sold.

In the third quarter of 2026 Indians bought 162 tonnes of gold and sold back just 19 tonnes of old gold. A record price became borrowing, not selling. Since May 2026 the import duty is back at 15%, and the World Gold Council estimates about 50 tonnes came in unofficially that quarter, against 3 tonnes a year earlier.

Why it matters. Gold imports cost about $72 bn in FY2025-26, roughly a fifth of India's merchandise trade deficit (gold figure via press, being confirmed). Small gold loans can now be up to 85% of the gold's value, so a dip in price can push pledged jewellery to auction. Only 392 of about 780 districts have mandatory hallmarking, and most gold already at home predates it.

Sources · WGC, India Focus Q3 2026 · Ministry of Commerce via PIB, Apr 2026 · RBI Gold Lending Directions 2025 · Rajya Sabha Q. 2602, Aug 2026

Key takeaways
  1. India bought 11% less gold by weight in 2025 but spent 30% more on it.
  2. Indian homes hold far more gold than the RBI, though the exact amount is an estimate with a wide range.
  3. Families are borrowing against gold and buying it as funds, rather than selling it.
  4. What gets missed: Indians pawn gold far more than they sell it, smuggling returned when the duty went back up, and gold imports are about a fifth of the trade deficit.
