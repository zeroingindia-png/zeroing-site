One stall is easy to miss. A whole lane is not.

Zeroing is a community of homegrown Indian brands, made in India and working towards sustainability, that open doors for each other. Before building it, we studied how others around the world have tried to help small businesses be seen. Here is what worked, what did not, and what we took from it.

What worked

Four efforts from India and abroad that changed how small makers are seen. Each one has a lesson we use.

  1. India, since 1981

    Dastkar bazaars

    Craftspeople come to the city, show how they work and sell straight to buyers. Laila Tyabji, Dastkar's chairperson, says the purpose is "to bring the consumer and the craftsperson together". Makers learn what buyers want, and buyers learn what a craft takes. Forty five years on, the bazaars still run.

    The lesson: put the maker and the buyer in the same room.

    Sources: Cocoa and Jasmine, interview with Laila Tyabji and YourStory, 2026

  2. United Kingdom, since 2013

    Small Business Saturday

    One shared day each December when the whole country is asked to shop at independent businesses. In 2020, 15.4 million people spent about £1.1 billion, the first time it passed a billion.

    The lesson: many small voices saying one thing at the same time are heard.

    Source: TheIndustry.fashion, with American Express data, December 2020

  3. Worldwide, since 2014

    Fashion Revolution

    Started after the Rana Plaza factory collapse in Bangladesh killed more than 1,100 garment workers. It asks one question, who made my clothes, in over 100 countries. Its index of the largest brands shows average transparency rising from 23% in 2021 to 26% in 2023, and in a 2019 survey of 5,000 European shoppers, 80% said brands should say where their clothes are made.

    The lesson: show who makes the thing and where.

    Source: FashionUnited, ten years of Fashion Revolution, 2024

  4. United States, studies since 2002

    The local multiplier

    Across Civic Economics studies, 52.9% of every purchase at an independent business is spent again locally, against less than 13.6% at a chain. Money spent with small makers keeps working in the place it was spent.

    The lesson: buying from each other is how a community gets stronger.

    Source: American Independent Business Alliance, summary of Civic Economics studies

What did not

Three efforts that had money, reach or the government behind them, and still left small sellers struggling.

  1. Worldwide, April 2022

    Etsy

    When Etsy raised its seller fee from 5% to 6.5%, thousands of sellers went on strike for a week and a petition passed 75,000 signatures. One seller called Etsy a near monopoly on handmade. Years of building on one platform left them with nowhere else to go.

    The lesson: every brand needs a shop and an audience it owns.

    Source: WBUR, April 2022

  2. India, from 2011

    Craftsvilla

    An online marketplace for artisans and designers that charged a 20% commission and raised over $50 million in 2015. Its first funding ran out by 2012, it later laid off staff, and it moved towards its own labels to protect margins.

    The lesson: grow at the pace of the makers, not the money.

    Sources: Wikipedia, Craftsvilla and Inside Retail Asia

  3. India, 2024 to 2025

    ONDC

    A government backed open network meant to let any small seller reach any buyer app. Retail orders fell from 65 lakh in October 2024 to 45 lakh in February 2025 as cash incentives were cut. Shoppers found it unclear, and there is no central place to resolve disputes.

    The lesson: being listed is not being found. People buy from names they trust.

    Source: Policy Circle, 2025

What we took from it

  • Every brand keeps its own shop.

    Brand pages here send you to the brand's own website and Instagram. Zeroing is a way in, never a checkout in the middle.

  • We show who makes it.

    Every brand page names the founder, the city and what they make. Independent Society goes further and records how the work is actually done.

  • We speak together.

    Brands present each conversation together and are credited on every episode, so one story reaches every brand's audience.

  • We grow at the makers' pace.

    New brands join through a conversation with the founder, and each one is introduced to the brands it can help.

What every brand shares

Four things a brand must be before it joins Zeroing.

  • Made in India.

    Designed and made here, from the materials and hands this country has. Every brand page says where.

  • Homegrown.

    Started and run by Indian founders, not a licence of a foreign label. You can meet the person behind it.

  • Working towards sustainability.

    Making things that last, with fewer shortcuts on materials, packaging and people. No brand here claims to be finished. Each page says what it is doing now and what comes next.

  • Ready to open doors.

    Every founder names one way they can help another brand in the community, before they join.
