What worked

Four efforts, in India and abroad, that got small makers seen. Each left a lesson we use.

  1. India, since 1981

    Dastkar bazaars

    Dastkar's bazaars bring craftspeople to the city to meet buyers. Laila Tyabji, Dastkar's chairperson, says the purpose is "to bring the consumer and the craftsperson together". Makers learn what buyers want, and buyers learn what a craft takes. Forty five years on, the bazaars still run.

    The lesson: put the maker and the buyer in the same room.

    Source: Cocoa and Jasmine, interview with Laila Tyabji

  2. United Kingdom, since 2013

    Small Business Saturday

    One shared day each December when the whole country is asked to shop at independent businesses. In 2020, 15.4 million people spent about £1.1 billion, the first time it passed a billion.

    The lesson: many small voices, saying one thing on one day, get heard.

    Source: TheIndustry.fashion, with American Express data, December 2020

  3. Worldwide, since 2014

    Fashion Revolution

    It began after the Rana Plaza factory collapse in Bangladesh killed more than 1,100 garment workers. Now it asks one question in over 100 countries. Who made my clothes? On its index of the largest brands, average transparency rose from 23% in 2021 to 26% in 2023. In a 2019 survey of 5,000 European shoppers, 80% said brands should say where their clothes are made.

    The lesson: show who makes the thing and where.

    Source: FashionUnited, ten years of Fashion Revolution, 2024

  4. United States, studies since 2002

    The local multiplier

    Across Civic Economics studies, 52.9% of every purchase at an independent business is spent again locally, against less than 13.6% at a chain. Money spent with small makers keeps working in the place it was spent.

    The lesson: buy from each other, and the money stays close.

    Source: American Independent Business Alliance, summary of Civic Economics studies

Where it went wrong

Three efforts with money, reach or the government behind them. Small sellers still struggled.

  1. Worldwide, April 2022

    Etsy

    When Etsy raised its seller fee from 5% to 6.5%, thousands of sellers went on strike for a week and a petition passed 75,000 signatures. One seller said Etsy has a monopoly on the handmade marketplace. Years of building on one platform left them with nowhere else to go.

    The lesson: every brand needs a shop and an audience it owns.

    Source: WBUR, April 2022

  2. India, from 2011

    Craftsvilla

    An online marketplace for artisans and designers, taking a 20% commission. Its first funding ran out by 2012. It raised over $50 million in 2015, later laid off staff, and moved towards its own labels to protect margins.

    The lesson: grow at the pace of the makers, not the money.

    Sources: Wikipedia, Craftsvilla and Inside Retail Asia

  3. India, 2024 to 2025

    ONDC

    A government backed open network meant to let any small seller reach any buyer app. Retail orders fell from 65 lakh in October 2024 to 45 lakh in February 2025 after cash incentives were reduced in some sectors and urban demand weakened. Policy Circle reports that shoppers found it unclear and that there is no single place to resolve disputes.

    The lesson: being listed is not being found. People buy from names they trust.

    Source: Policy Circle, 2025

What we took from it

  • Every brand keeps its own shop.

    Brand pages here send you to the brand's own website and Instagram. Zeroing is a way in, never a checkout in the middle.

  • We show who makes it.

    Every brand page says what they make, and names the founder and the city once the founder confirms them. Independent Society goes further, into how the work really gets done.

  • We speak together.

    Brands present each conversation together, and the brands that present it are credited on it. One story reaches each of their audiences.

  • We grow at the makers' pace.

    New brands join through a conversation with the founder, and each one is introduced to the brands it can help.

What every brand shares

Four things a brand must be before it joins Zeroing.

  • Made in India.

    Designed and made in India, as the founder confirms when applying. Every brand page shows where, once the founder has told us.

  • Homegrown.

    Started and run by Indian founders, not a licence of a foreign label. You can meet the person behind it.

  • Working towards sustainability.

    A brand joins by naming one real step on materials, packaging or making, taken or planned. We call no brand sustainable. Each page shows what the founder has told us, or says it is still to come.

  • Ready to open doors.

    Before joining, every founder names one door they can open for another brand.
